Slice 0.2.4 is out. Until now, only iPhone recordings could go on a 3D device. Now your screen recordings can too: turn on the 3D tab and your recording plays on a 3D MacBook, right there in the editor.

## Two MacBooks

Pick a **MacBook Air 15** in Space Gray, Silver, Starlight or Midnight, or a **MacBook Neo** in yellow, silver, blue, pink or green. Your recording plays on the screen, with the bezel, notch and keyboard around it. Your recording itself is never changed, so you can turn 3D off again at any time.

## Open and close the lid

Use the **Lid** slider to set how far open the MacBook stands, from closed to leaning back. To animate it, move the playhead to where you want the move to start and click **Open** or **Close**. Slice adds the keyframes for you, and the lid swings open or shut over just over a second. It's a nice way to start or end a video.

## Poses made for a laptop

The one-click poses now fit the device you picked. A MacBook gets **Front**, **Turned**, **Hero**, **Side**, **From above** and **Low**, and starts tipped slightly towards you so the keyboard shows. Everything else works the same as the 3D iPhone: turn, tilt, rotate and resize it, add keyframes to move it over time, and export it in every shape.

## Install or update

Run this in Terminal:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://slice.rit3zh.dev/install.sh | bash
```

Or [download Slice.dmg](https://github.com/rit3zh/slice/releases/latest) and drag Slice to Applications. If macOS says it can't verify Slice, click **Done**, then go to **System Settings › Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**. You only need to do this once.

Slice needs macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon.
